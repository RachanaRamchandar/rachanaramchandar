01 / INTRODUCTION

A little about me.

I'm an undergraduate researcher at the intersection of natural language processing, human–computer interaction, and computational cognitive science.

I am interested in the behaviors that conventional scores can miss: when a model asks a useful question, when it produces a genuinely novel idea, and when its answers conceal contradictory commitments. I work to turn those questions into careful, reproducible evaluations—and eventually into interventions that make AI systems more reliable.

Alongside this work, I use machine learning in cultural heritage and healthcare contexts. I enjoy the process of moving from an interesting question to a well-defined experiment, and I value research that is both technically rigorous and grounded in real-world use.

02 / RESEARCH

Questions that drive my work.

My research examines model behavior beyond surface-level fluency or accuracy, with a focus on interpretable and reproducible evaluation.

01
↗

Curiosity & information seeking

When should a model ask a question instead of answering? I study useful probing in human–LLM conversations and how to distinguish epistemic curiosity from generic question asking.

02
≠

Epistemic behavior & reasoning

How do contradictory commitments emerge across stochastic generations? I investigate trajectory instability, semantic response clusters, and contradictions between final answers.

03
✳

Computational creativity

When does creative-looking output reflect meaningful exploration? I examine failure modes in frontier models performing creative tasks and design diagnostics for targeted improvement.

04
◎

Human-centered evaluation

How can benchmarks tell us why systems succeed or fail? I combine metric design, human evaluation, uncertainty analysis, and reproducible experimental protocols.
